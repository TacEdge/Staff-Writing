# 01 Principles and language

Source: DFI 5.1 Part 1 Chapter 1 (1.1.1–1.1.11) and Chapter 2 Sections 1–2
(1.2.1–1.2.15). Part 4 drafting rules are cited where they add to these.

## 1. Principles of Defence Force writing

| Rule | Tag | Cite |
|---|---|---|
| All Defence Force writing must be correctly formatted, error-free, factual and grammatically correct; contain only relevant information; use common vocabulary; use well-defined paragraphs and easy-to-read sentences; and be technically correct before signature. | [M] | 1.2.3b |
| Writers are to use the appropriate templates and conform to DFI 5.1 styles and conventions. | [M] | 1.2.3e |
| Be accurate and brief; structure logically; distinguish fact from opinion and say whose opinion it is; qualify assumptions; no weasel words or double-speak. | [M]/[D] | 1.2.3d, f |
| Guiding principles: direct and courteous; plain language; sparing abbreviations; active verbs; average sentence about 20 words with one main point; break up text with paragraphs; avoid jargon, clichés, padding, euphemisms, tautologies, foreign words, fashionable words. | [D] | 1.2.3g(1)–(16) |
| Plain English: a reader should understand and act on the document after one reading. | [D] | 1.2.1a |
| Everything written can be released under the OIA. Write nothing that cannot be defended in public or that compromises personal privacy. | [M] | 1.1.9d, 1.1.10h |
| Email is the primary means of day-to-day business. A minute is required where the content seeks a decision on finance or resources or on complex issues, or presents a complex report. | [M] | 1.1.3d(1)–(2) |

## 2. Directive language (1.2.6)

| Term | Meaning | Tag |
|---|---|---|
| is to / are to / must / must not | Imperative. Action or duty imposed; no alternative interpretation. Used in DFOs, CDF Directives and other non-discretionary direction. | [M] |
| may | Permissive; action is discretionary. | [M] |
| shall / will | Ambiguous. Use sparingly in orders, directions and instructions. | [D] |
| should | Requirement of higher authority with some discretion. | [M] |
| intend | Will be carried out unless countermanded. | [M] |
| propose | Will not be undertaken unless approved. | [M] |
| request (senior to junior) | A polite form of direction; to be read as direction. | [M] |

- DFOs must be written in mandatory language (3.2.1d(1), 4.3.2b). Orders and
  instructions are written in the present tense (4.3.16b(3)). In DFIs, mandatory
  language must be clearly distinguishable from guidance (4.3.4f). [M]
- Operational directive tasks use "you are to", "be prepared to (BPT)" and
  "on order" (3.2.17(4)(b)). [M]
- "Command intent" or "CDF intent" is generally used only in operational
  directives, and may be used in directives and AIs (fn 16). [D]

## 3. Language and spelling

| Rule | Tag | Cite |
|---|---|---|
| Write primarily in English. Māori terms are encouraged where appropriate; Māori content must be culturally and linguistically correct, with correct macrons (Ā ā Ē ē Ī ī Ō ō Ū ū). In te reo Māori the noun does not change in the plural. | [M] | 1.1.4 |
| Primary dictionary: The New Zealand Oxford Dictionary. Secondary: Shorter Oxford English Dictionary and Collins English Thesaurus. Māori: Te Aka. Where the NZDF customised MS Office dictionary differs from the NZOD, the NZDF dictionary prevails. | [M] | 1.1.5, 1.2.5c |
| Set the MS Office language to English (New Zealand). | [M] | 1.2.5b |
| Use the first dictionary form; use -ise not -ize; NZ spelling (colour, programme); follow an organisation's own spelling of its proper name (World Health Organization); use the first-listed plural. | [M] | 1.2.5c(1) |
| e-words take a hyphen (e-learning). Exception: email. | [D] | 1.2.5c(2) |
| Terminology must match the authorised sources in the order listed in 1.1.6b. Do not substitute an authorised term unless the different meaning is intended and explained. | [M] | 1.1.6 |
| Avoid military jargon unless the reader is known to understand it, and always for external readers. | [M] | 1.1.6e |
| Use gender-neutral language unless a specific gender is intended (techniques in 1.1.7b(1)–(6)). Use non-discriminatory language. | [M] | 1.1.7 |
| Supplementary references when DFI is silent, in this order: Cabinet Manual; Chicago Manual of Style; Grammar Monster; Smashing Grammar. | [D] | 1.1.8 |

## 4. Punctuation (1.2.7)

| Rule | Tag |
|---|---|
| One space after a full stop or question mark. | [M] |
| No exclamation marks. | [M] |
| No full stops in initials or honorific decorations, including in signature blocks. A comma may separate decorations (Captain AJ Gillespie DSD, MNZM, RNZN (rtd)). | [M] |
| No serial (Oxford) comma unless it is needed for clarity. | [M] |
| No punctuation within or after abbreviations or dates except as normal sentence structure requires (eg, etc, 9mm, AL). | [M] |
| Colon: may introduce important information. One space after. | [D] |
| Semicolon: may join closely related elements and is used in some vertical lists. One space after. | [D] |
| **Em dash (—):** limited to DFOs, DFIs and other legislative-style drafting, chiefly to introduce lists. No space before it. **Not used in correspondence or administrative documentation.** See [A-07] for the templates that conflict with this. | [M] |
| En dash (–), unspaced, for ranges (10–12, 2–4 Sep 16) and for proper nouns of equal value (New Zealand–Australia). | [M] |
| Hyphens, unspaced, to clarify compounds (follow the NZOD). **Turn off MS Word auto-hyphenation.** | [M] |
| Forward slash for alternatives, with no spaces. | [M] |
| Footnote reference marks go after punctuation, except hyphens, en and em dashes, and text inside parentheses. | [M] |
| Square brackets for editorial insertions, [sic] and [emphasis added]. | [M] |
| Apostrophes are not used in plurals of abbreviations (COs). Use them only for possession (CDF's). | [M] |

## 5. Abbreviations, acronyms and initialisms (1.2.8)

| Rule | Tag | Cite |
|---|---|---|
| Avoid shortened forms in DFOs. Where unique terms are needed, define them as the Chief Parliamentary Counsel requires. | [D] | 1.2.8b |
| Meaning must be obvious or in an authorised glossary. Spell the term out in full with the abbreviation in brackets at first use, **and again in each chapter** of a publication. Use it consistently thereafter. | [M] | 1.2.8c, e(5) |
| Do not abbreviate if the term is used only once. eg, ie and etc may be used sparingly without introduction. | [M] | 1.2.8e(3)–(4) |
| Do not use abbreviations where the tone makes them inappropriate (citations, laudatory letters, condolence letters). | [M] | 1.2.8d |
| ANZAC refers only to the Corps; otherwise write Anzac. | [M] | 1.2.8e(1) |
| Write names of countries in full, except for currency and in tables. | [D] | 1.2.8e(2) |
| An abbreviated form does not need initial capitals because the original term has them (commanding officers → COs). | [M] | 1.2.8e(6) |
| Show retired status as (rtd) after any honorifics. | [M] | 1.2.8e(8) |
| Appointment titles are abbreviated in administrative documentation, minute addressees, directives and AIs. | [M] | 2.1.11(5), 2.2.3(6), 3.2.11(9), 3.2.22a(1) |
| Avoid Service abbreviations in formal letters (civilian ones such as Dr, JP and MP are acceptable). Spell the term in full at first use. | [M] | 2.1.16(14) |
| Do not use acronyms in correspondence to Ministers unless the Minister prefers them. Do not use abbreviations in NTM subject headings or recommendations. | [M] | 2.3.6a, 2.3.8(7), Fig 2-27 |

## 6. Capitalisation (1.2.9)

| Rule | Tag |
|---|---|
| Keep capitals to a minimum. | [M] |
| Capitalise proper nouns, titles, honorifics, institutions, courses, buildings, official policies and similar. | [M] |
| Ship, operation and exercise names in full capitals: HMNZS TE MANA, Operation SOUTHERN WATCH, Exercise SOUTHERN KATIPO. | [M] |
| Abbreviated ranks and titles in capitals in signature blocks and internal body text (MGEN, CAPT, SSGT, MR). | [M] |
| Abbreviations and acronyms in capitals unless the NZOD shows otherwise. | [M] |
| **Subject headings, security classifications and endorsement markings in UPPER CASE.** | [M] |
| Body text and end-matter headings, including enclosure and annex headings, in sentence case. | [M] |
| Main headings capitalise primary words (an exception to sentence case, 1.2.17(2)). | [M] |

## 8. Numbers and symbols (1.2.13)

| Rule | Tag |
|---|---|
| Spell out numbers below 10; use numerals for 10 and above. Ordinals follow the same rule. No superscript ordinals (21st). | [M] |
| Write "one" where the numeral 1 could be confused with a letter, and "zero" instead of 0. | [M] |
| Spell out a number at the start of a sentence. | [M] |
| Decimals: 4.55. Put a zero before the point when the value is less than one: 0.93. | [M] |
| Thousands separated by commas: 4,000. | [M] |
| Form, part and catalogue numbers keep their original format. | [M] |
| Phone numbers: (0n) nnn nnnn; overseas +64 (0)n nnn nnnn; internal DTelN (nnn) nnnn. | [M] |
| Write "per cent", not %, in text and table column headings. | [M] |
| Sums of money: words with the figure in brackets: twelve thousand dollars ($12,000). Millions: $12 million or $2.7 million. Avoid starting a sentence with a sum. | [M] |
| Currency: country code, symbol, amount, no spaces: NZD$400, EUR€10,000. Write the currency word in lower case (dollar). | [M] |
| Units: a recognised symbol or the full word, with a non-breaking space between number and unit (27.8 kg, 50 km/h). | [M] |

Note: 1.2.13(1) and (3) can conflict ("twelve" is spelt out in the (3) example).
See [A-29].

## 9. Emphasis, italics, quotations

| Rule | Tag | Cite |
|---|---|---|
| Keep emphasis to a minimum. It is used only in email, minutes, DFIs and DMs. Use underline, bold or 'single quotes', never double quotes. | [M] | 1.2.11 |
| In a recommendations paragraph, put the action verb in **bold** (**note**, **agree**, **approve**). | [M] | 1.2.11(2); Fig 2-3, 2-34 |
| Email may use limited bold, colour or highlighting for emphasis. | [D] | fn 31 |
| Italics may be used for titles of complete works, trademarks, uncommon foreign words or clichés, and repeated quotations. | [D] | 1.2.12 |
| Quotations must be accurate and footnoted on the same page. Keep them to three lines or fewer, in 'single quotes', with "double quotes" only inside a quotation. Set them off by one line, indented, "one half point lower font", italics optional. See [A-09]. | [M] | 1.2.14 |

## 10. References (1.2.15, 1.2.19, Table 1-1)

- Cite only key principal references at the start of a document, and they must
  be referred to in the text. Put all other references in footnotes. [M]
- No endnotes or bibliography, except in academic writing. [M]
  (Professional literature follows the Chicago Manual of Style instead: 2.2.25.)
- Citation formats by type (Table 1-1) [M]:

| Type | Format | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Documents | subject or file reference, date | HQ2 LFG 4500/1, 5 Apr 16 |
| Signals | originator, signal indicator code, DTG | HQNZDF (NZVY) ZAA/ZAX 142359ZOCT17 |
| Extracts | principal document, chapter, section, paragraph | DFO 4, chapter 7, section 3, paragraph 7.44 |
| Maps | series, sheet, name, edition, scale | NZMS 317, Sheet 1, WAIOURU, Edition 1, 1:50 000 |
| Charts | number, title, edition | NZ200023, South Pacific Ocean - New Zealand - North Island, Edition 4 |
| Orders, directions, instructions | serial number and title | FRAGO 002 to CDF Operational Directive 18/2020 |
| Telephone conversations and discussions | type, persons, date-time | Telecon SGT King/SSGT Mathis, 15 1300 Jun 20 |
| Hyperlinks | allowed in emails and online publications; **not in minutes or letters** | |
| Between publications | document, part, chapter, section (no lower than section) | DFI 5.1 part 1, chapter 2, section 2 |

## 11. Official information and security (content)

- Every document is official information and may be released under the OIA.
  It must meet the Privacy Act 2020. [M] 1.1.9, 1.1.10h
- A document's classification is that of its most highly classified content.
  The body must not contain, or refer to, material classified higher than the
  document. [M] 2.1.11(10), (12); 2.3.9c
- Aim for the lowest possible classification. [D] 2.1.11(12)
