- Preprinted letterhead or an authorised crest is used only for CDF Directives,
  COS and COMJFNZ directives, letters to NZDF members, external correspondence
  and publication title pages. [M] 1.1.3d(6)
- The letterhead must match the originating command, unit or directorate.
  [M] 1.1.3d(9)
- No variation to the badge is authorised (other than CDF's gold leaf).
  [M] 1.2.27e
- **Placement:** 1.2.18a says the header contains no badge, and the badge goes
  immediately below the header in line with the address block. 2.1.16(3) says
  the visual identifier goes "in the top left of the header" on the first page
  of a letter. See [A-14]. In every template the badge is top left and the
  address block top right.
- **Artwork:** Annex 1C shows the badges and logos, but production artwork must
  come from the NZDF identity standards (DFI 0.103 per 1.1.3f/4.1.7b; DFI 3.1
  per 1.2.26: see [A-32]). Assets are a user-supplied input (register T-05).

## 2. Protective markings

| Rule | Tag | Cite |
|---|---|---|
| Three kinds of protective marking: security classification, endorsement marking, compartmented marking (compartmented markings are outside DFI 5.1). | [M] | 1.1.10c |
| An endorsement marking is never used without a security classification. | [M] | 1.1.10f |
| Placement: header first line = classification, then endorsement; footer = endorsement, then classification on the last line. Centred, bold, upper case, Calibri 12 pt, on **every page**, including annexes. | [M] | 1.2.16(5), 1.2.18 |
| Unclassified, with no reference to classified material: no marking required. | [M] | fn 24 |
| Any correspondence with the Minister of Defence: **IN-CONFIDENCE** on all pages. Templates show "minimum of IN-CONFIDENCE". | [M] | 2.3.6f; Figs 2-26 to 2-35 |
| Defence Weekly Report: IN-CONFIDENCE always; RESTRICTED if the content requires it; Restricted is the maximum. | [M] | 2.3.12c |
| Publications: markings on all pages, including front and back covers. | [M] | 4.4.7a(2) |
| Email: Restricted, Sensitive or In-Confidence goes in the subject line **and** the first line. External email subject: "UNCLASSIFIED Subject title". | [M] | 2.1.8(3) |
| Formal letter example of a combined marking: "UNCLASSIFIED – IN-CONFIDENCE". | [M] | 2.1.16(1) |
| Visit report: classified no lower than the visit or activity. | [M] | 2.2.10(1) |
| Paragraph grading indicators: (U), (R), (C) after the paragraph number, only where paragraphs may be used separately. | [D] | 2.1.11(12) |
| Meeting minutes may mark individual topics, for example "5. (R) Subject". The overall marking is the highest used. | [M] | 2.2.15d; Figs 2-20, 2-22 |
| Full rules for the classification system and for classified page numbering are in DFO 51 and the PSR (**not held**). | – | 1.1.10g; see [A-13], [A-31] |

Markings that appear in DFI 5.1 (the only vocabulary the renderer accepts until
[A-13] is resolved): UNCLASSIFIED, IN-CONFIDENCE (IN CONFIDENCE in Fig 2-13),
STAFF-IN-CONFIDENCE (Fig 3-3), SENSITIVE (2.1.8(3)), RESTRICTED, CONFIDENTIAL
(paragraph grading (C), 4.2.12), SECRET and TOP SECRET (4.2.12(3)).
