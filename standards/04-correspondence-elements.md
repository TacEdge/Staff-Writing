# 04 Correspondence elements

These are the building blocks shared by minutes, submissions, letters,
administrative documents, ministerial notes and directives. Each template lists
which blocks it uses, in what order, and any per-type variant.

Source: DFI 5.1 1.2.19, 1.2.21, 2.1.3, 2.1.11, 2.1.16–2.1.18, 2.2.3, Annex 1A,
and the template annexes.

## 2. Signature blocks (1.2.21 plus per-type rules)

### Common rules

| Rule | Tag | Cite |
|---|---|---|
| Every document is authorised by a signature. The signatory takes responsibility for the content and handling. | [M] | 1.2.21a |
| A person signing for another writes "for" in lower case in the margin to the left of the signature block. | [M] | 1.2.21b, 2.1.11(15) |
| **Never put a signature block on a page without text. At least two lines of text must come before it.** | [M] | 1.2.21c |
| The block is set **six lines below** the last line of text. See [A-08]. | [M] | 1.2.21c |
| No full stops in initials or decorations. Abbreviated ranks in capitals. | [M] | 1.2.7(3), 1.2.9(4) |
| Drafts use the block of the person who will sign the final. | [M] | 1.2.22(4) |

Notes:
- Who may sign is a content and validation rule. External letters: only CDF,
  COS, or a senior commander or executive with delegated authority (2.1.16(16)).
  CDF Directives: CDF, or VCDF or CoS HQNZDF in urgent cases (3.2.11(7)).
  Operational directives: CDF or VCDF (3.2.18(7)). Joint notes: CDF **and**
  Secretary of Defence (2.3.8(9)). Visit reports: the senior member of the party
  or of the host unit (2.2.10(7)). AIs: a person with delegated authority
  (3.2.22b).
- The rank abbreviation list is not in DFI 5.1 (only examples in fn 19). The
  examples are themselves inconsistent (MAJGEN in Fig 2-18 vs MGEN in fn 19).
  See [A-10].

## 3. Salutation and complimentary close (letters only)

Internal letters (2.1.17):
- Exactly one addressee, addressed by **name** (not appointment). The address
  block shows the name and the appointment or post. [M]
- Letter of admonition: rank, initials and surname, then appointment. No
  salutation and no close; the writer signs over the block. [M]
- Congratulatory and condolence letters: the salutation is usually
  handwritten. "In most other formal letters there is no salutation." See
  [A-15]. [D]
- "Dear Colonel Smith" → "Yours faithfully"; "Dear Richard" → "Yours sincerely".
  No comma after the salutation or close. A handwritten salutation means a
  handwritten close. [M]

External letters (2.1.18):
- Salutation typed or handwritten, with no comma. [M]
- Known to the writer: first name (and rank if appropriate) → "Yours sincerely". [M]
- Name and pronouns known: "Dear Mr/Mrs/Ms/Miss [last name]". Pronouns not
  known: "Dear [first name] [last name]" → "Yours faithfully". [M]
- Name unknown: "Dear Sir", "Dear Madam" or "Dear Sir or Madam" → "Yours
  faithfully". [M]
- Unnamed recipient addressed by job title: **no greeting and no close**. No
  greeting means no close. [M]
- Former members of the Armed Forces: write the rank in full if appropriate. [D]

## 4. Addressee block (letters)

- Post-nominals at the writer's discretion. Day-to-day business may be
  addressed by job title. Personal matters must use the person's name.
  [M] 2.1.16(7)
- External example layout: name, organisation, street, TOWN POSTCODE (town in
  capitals). [T] Fig 2-10
